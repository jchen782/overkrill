Astana[a] is the capital city of Kazakhstan. With a population of 1.7 million within the city limits, it is the second-largest in the country after Almaty, the national capital until 1997.[14] The city lies on the banks of the Ishim river in the north of Kazakhstan and is located within the Akmola Region but is self-administering. Initially founded as Aqmoly in 1830, the city was later renamed Akmolinsk, Tselinograd, and Aqmola before adopting the name Astana in 1998, or "capital" in Kazakh.[15] In 2019, the city adopted the name Nur-Sultan in honor of former president Nursultan Nazarbayev, but it reverted to the name Astana in 2022.
<br>
Astana's history includes growth, rapidly expanding after becoming the capital.[16][17] The decision to turn Astana into a modern and planned city was guided by Kisho Kurokawa.[17] It has become an important center for culture, education, and commerce in Kazakhstan and Central Asia.[18][19][20]
<br>
Akmoly was founded in 1830, named after a local landmark. "Ақ мола" (Aqmola) means "white grave" or "white tomb" in Kazakh,[21] although this theory is not universally accepted.[1] In 1832, it was granted town status and renamed Akmolinsk.[1] In 1961, under Nikita Khrushchev, it was renamed Tselinograd, Russian for "City of Virgin Lands".[22] In 1991, following Kazakhstan's independence, the name was changed to Akmola.[22]
<br>
In December 1997, the city replaced Almaty as the capital of Kazakhstan, and in May 1998, it was renamed Astana, which means "capital city" in Kazakh.[23] In March 2019, the capital was renamed to Nur-Sultan (/ˌnʊərsʊlˈtɑːn/;[10]Kazakh: Нұр-Сұлтан / Nūr-Sūltan [ˌnʊrsʊɫˈtɑn]) in honor of the long-ruling President Nursultan Nazarbayev, shortly after his resignation.[24][25] In September 2022, President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev signed a constitutional amendment to revert to the name Astana.[26][27] As of 2022[update], it holds the Guinness World Record for the capital city with the most name changes in modern times.[22]
